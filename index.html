Impact Report - Year ended 31 March 2026

A word from the trustees

Kia ora,

You are reading Biome Trust Aotearoa's first impact report. It shares some of the work we supported during the year ended 31 March 2026, and what we learned along the way.

This report reflects a year of continued evolution for Biome Trust Aotearoa: growing our giving, deepening relationships with the people and organisations we support, and becoming clearer about the role we can play beyond individual grants.

The year also marked an important transition for Ma Earth, which became an independent charitable foundation after growing out of Biome Trust's experience in grantmaking and land stewardship.

We remain a learning organisation. In the year ahead, we will continue giving, listening and collaborating, while looking for places where Biome Trust can make a useful contribution to a healthier and more flourishing future.

Acknowledgements

We are deeply grateful to the people and organisations who contributed to Biome Trust's work this year. In particular, we thank our partners for the work they lead, the knowledge and experience they share, and the trust that underpins our relationships.

We acknowledge tangata whenua and the wider people of Aotearoa New Zealand, and our shared interest in a healthy and flourishing society.

We also thank our trustees, team, advisers, collaborators and community members for their care, skill and practical support. The progress reflected in this report is collective, and we are grateful to everyone who helped make it possible.

Four principles that guide our giving

01

Trust people close to the work

Give capable leaders room to lead and keep funding processes simple and appropriate to the work.

02

Build relationships beyond the grant

Invest in relationships, shared learning and useful connections — not only funding.

03

Stay curious

04

Value depth as well as scale

Look for evidence and honest reflection, while recognising that meaningful, place-based change is not always easy to measure.

In summary

We believe good funding starts with trusting people close to the work. We aim to give capable leaders room to lead, build strong relationships and keep learning alongside them.

Mangaroa Farms

Wholly owned by Biome Trust, Mangaroa Farms is a charitable entity spanning 1,222 hectares in the Mangaroa river catchment, 40 minutes north of Wellington. With a growing team, this living laboratory focuses on ecological resilience, accessible kai and community education.

This year, Mangaroa Farms built its first ecological baseline, and measured the impact of a decade of work.

Our own initiatives

Ma Earth grew out of Biome Trust's experience of a persistent funding gap: major funders struggle to find and confidently support high-quality grassroots nature regeneration and conservation projects, while those projects struggle to access major donors.

Ma Earth is building simpler infrastructure for both. It helps funders discover strong projects that have passed due diligence and selection, and support them without building bilateral relationships with each project.

For grassroots projects, Ma Earth opens access to new funding, including community crowdfunding amplified by philanthropic matching, and lets them share information once with multiple funders while retaining control of their data.

Observations

Liquidity vs deeper mission alignment

Biome Trust intends to spend down its endowment over the coming years, favouring liquidity and lower risk. That preserves flexibility, but the most mission-aligned opportunities tend to be private and illiquid.

Learn before scaling private investment

Private funds can run 10–12 years, and outcomes may take 5–10 years to assess. That slow feedback cycle is challenging for a spend-down endowment, so we scale commitments thoughtfully.

Think globally

We manage the portfolio as a whole across fund holders and jurisdictions, seeking the best opportunities for meaningful impact rather than allocating capital by geography alone.
